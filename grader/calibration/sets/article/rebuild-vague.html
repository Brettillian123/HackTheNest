All Aboard: Europe's Sleeper Train Revival

How overnight rail went from relic to the hottest ticket in travel
Written by Staff Writer | Updated this week

For decades, the night train seemed destined for the history books. Cheap flights made it easy to cross the continent in a couple of hours, and rail operators quietly retired their sleeper carriages one route at a time.

But something unexpected has happened. Across Europe, overnight trains are making a remarkable comeback, driven by climate concerns, a desire for slower travel, and a new generation of operators betting big on the romance of the rails.

"There's something magical about falling asleep in Vienna and waking up in Venice."

A Growing Network

The numbers tell the story. Since 2020, dozens of new sleeper connections have been launched or announced, linking major capitals and holiday destinations alike.

Whether you're chasing sustainability or simply nostalgia, there has never been a better time to travel by night.
