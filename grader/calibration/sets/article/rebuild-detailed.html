The Quiet Return of the Night Train

By Hannah Okafor · November 9, 2026 · 8 min read

Ten years ago, the sleeper train looked finished. Budget airlines had taken the routes, the carriages were old, and operators were cutting services every winter. Today new lines open every season, and many of them sell out weeks in advance.

What changed was not the trains themselves but the travellers. Families, remote workers and people avoiding short flights now treat a night on the rails as part of the trip rather than a cost of it.

"You go to sleep in one country and wake up in another. No airport has ever given me that."

Checking the timetable yourself

Most operators publish their timetables as open data. A short query is enough to find every overnight departure:

SELECT route, depart, arrive FROM departures WHERE depart >= '20:00' AND overnight = true ORDER BY depart;

How to book a good berth

  1. Book as soon as the season opens, usually ninety days ahead.
  2. Choose a lower berth if you want an easy climb at night.
  3. Bring a small bag; there is little room for suitcases.
  4. Check whether breakfast is included before you pay extra.
