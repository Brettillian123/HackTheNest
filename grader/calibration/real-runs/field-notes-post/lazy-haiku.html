What we learned from a year of four-day weeks

By Maya Ortiz · March 4, 2026 · 7 min read

Last March we tried something our board called “brave” and our accountant called “unwise”: every team would work Monday to Thursday, with Friday off and no cut in pay. We promised to measure everything and to stop if it didn’t work.

We expected output to fall. Instead, most teams shipped slightly more, and the number of late projects dropped by a third. The biggest change was not speed but focus: people stopped filling Fridays with meetings that could have been an email.

“I get more done in four focused days than I used to in five scattered ones.”

That line came from our support lead, and it summed up the survey.

Nobody wanted to go back.

Even the two teams whose numbers dipped asked to keep the schedule and fix the problems instead.
